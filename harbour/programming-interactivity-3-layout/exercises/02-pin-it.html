Act 2 · Position

Pin it

Six things that need pinning. Press ◌ target in an editor to show a red chalk outline where the thing should end up. Cases 1–3 and 5 in class; 4 and 6 if you finish early. Then the same code goes into your project in the finale.

The rule of the act. absolute is measured from the nearest positioned ancestor (anything but static). No positioned ancestor → the first screen of the page. So: position: relative on the parent, position: absolute on the child.

1

The stamp

The Sold out stamps look like text. They should sit on the corner of the card, like a real stamp.

Pin the badge (.badge, inside a .vendor-card) so its top edge is 12px above the card and its right edge 16px in from the card's right edge. Rotate it if you like (transform: rotate(8deg)) — rotation doesn't count against you. Place it with top and right: translate only moves the paint, not the box.

Why it works

position: absolute takes the badge out of the flow (the card closes the gap it leaves) and measures top/right from the nearest positioned ancestor. Without position: relative on the card, that ancestor is the page — try removing it and watch the stamp fly.

.vendor-card { position: relative; }
.badge {
  position: absolute;
  top: -12px;
  right: 16px;
  transform: rotate(8deg);
}
2

The menu that runs away

Scroll down and the menu is gone. It should stay at the top.

Make the header (.site-header) stick to the top of the window while you scroll. Then scroll the preview past the Sold out card and look closely: who is painted on top? Fix that too.

Sticky doesn't stick? ① no top value ② its parent is only as tall as it is (sticky can't leave its parent) ③ an ancestor has overflow: hidden.

Why it works

sticky behaves like normal flow until the element would scroll past top: 0; then it sticks. Without top it never sticks. The stamp is positioned and comes later in the HTML, so it's painted on top of the header. z-index: 1 (on a positioned element) lifts the header above it.

.site-header {
  position: sticky;
  top: 0;
  z-index: 1;
}
3

You are here

The 📍 sits under the map instead of on it, at the entrance.

Put the pin (.you-are-here) 16px from the left and 16px from the bottom of the map (.market-map).

Why it works

Same pattern as the stamp: the map is the anchor. bottom and left measure from the bottom and left edges of the anchor's padding box.

.market-map { position: relative; }
.you-are-here {
  position: absolute;
  left: 16px;
  bottom: 16px;
}
4 · bonus

Back to top

The round “back to top” button should always sit in the bottom-right corner of the screen, even while scrolling.

Pin it (.to-top) 24px from the right and bottom edges of the window. Scroll the preview to test.

Why it works

fixed is measured from the window (the viewport), so it doesn't move when the page scrolls. Its parent's position doesn't matter.

.to-top {
  position: fixed;
  right: 24px;
  bottom: 24px;
}
5

z-index: 9999 still loses

The churros menu opens UNDER the header — even though it already has z-index: 9999.

The menu (.dropdown) must paint above the header (.top). Rules: don't touch the header, keep the card's transform (it animates on hover), don't move the menu. Why does 9999 lose to 10?

Why it works

transform turns the card into a stacking context (and the dropdown's containing block). Inside it, 9999 only ranks the dropdown among the card's children; the whole card paints as one layer at level 0, below the header's 10. Lift the context: give the card position: relative; z-index: 11. (Removing the transform would work too — but the rules forbade it.)

.vendor-card {
  position: relative;
  z-index: 11;
}
6 · bonus

The fixed button that scrolls away

The ↑ button is position: fixed (read-only), but it scrolls away with the page. Find the culprit in the HTML tab and make the button stick to the window's bottom-right corner again. You may not change .to-top.

Why it works

An ancestor with transform (also filter, perspective, contain: paint, will-change: transform) becomes the containing block for its fixed descendants. In real projects: render modals and toasts at the end of body, outside animated wrappers — or use a <dialog> opened with showModal(), which goes to the browser's top layer, above every stacking context.

.shell { transform: none; }
