The market jam

Five groups, five features for the Harbour Night Market, each one HTML + CSS + JavaScript. Thirty minutes to build, two and a half to show the class. Today's whole lecture, in one feature.

Set up (2 minutes)

  1. One laptop per group drives. Copy 04-responsive-dom/project/jam/ out of the repo (or unzip jam.zip) and open its index.html through a local server (npx live-server).
  2. It's the finished page from today, with two empty files linked: css/feature.css and js/feature.js. Your work goes there, plus the HTML your feature needs in index.html.
  3. Open your task's brief on a second laptop, for the people who aren't typing.

How you work

  • Discuss the solution together first (minutes 0–5): what you find, which event you listen for, what you change, and the CSS that draws it.
  • One person drives: types on the group's laptop.
  • Everyone else reviews and verifies: reads along and spots mistakes, checks the brief, tests on a phone (live-server's Network URL), with the keyboard only, after a reload.
  • Decide early who presents.

The five tasks (and two spares)

  • Task 1 · ★★☆Light, auto, darkA three-way switch in the header: ☀️ light, 🌗 auto (follow my system), 🌙 dark. The choice is remembered.
  • Task 2 · ★★☆My nightA ♡ on every stall. Save the ones you want to visit, see how many you saved, and show only those.
  • Task 3 · ★★☆What's on?A time slider above the schedule: pick a time, see what's on, and the schedule fits a phone.
  • Task 4 · ★★★Ticket wizardThe ticket form, one step at a time: You → Your visit → Check. A progress strip on top, and a review of every answer before Reserve.
  • Task 5 · ★★☆The map talksClick a zone on the market map and a panel tells you what's there. On a phone, the panel slides up from the bottom.
  • Spare task · ★☆☆Find a stallA search box above the vendors that filters the cards as you type, and says how many match.
  • Spare task · ★★★Register a stallA form under the vendors: a new stall fills in its name, category and one line, and its card appears in the list, filters and all.

The 30 minutes

0–5 min · planRead the brief together. Agree on the solution before anyone types.
5–20 · buildMust-haves only. Commit to one approach.
20–27 · verifyOn a phone. Keyboard only. A reload.
27–30 · demoStop coding. Rehearse your 2½ minutes once.

Presenting

Your 2½ minutes
  1. Demo it at phone width and at desktop width (device toolbar on the projector).
  2. The code: what you find, which event you listen for, what you change. Show those lines of feature.js.
  3. One CSS decision: the query, the variable or the selector that does the drawing.
  4. One bug you hit, and how you found it.

The class asks one question per group. Good questions: "what happens if…?", "why that selector?", "what would you change with ten more minutes?"
