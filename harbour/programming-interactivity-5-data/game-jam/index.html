Game jam

The game jam

Six groups, six small games, each built from three empty files. No market today. Every game works the way the market did at the end of Lecture 5: one state object, and a render() that draws the page from it.

The one rule

The DOM is output. An event changes state, then calls render(). render() reads state and draws everything. Nothing in your game logic reads the page: no textContent, no classList.contains to decide who won.

Set up (2 minutes)

  1. One laptop per group drives. Copy 05-data/project/game-jam/ out of the repo (or unzip game-jam.zip) and serve it (npx live-server).
  2. Three files: index.html, style.css, script.js. script.js has the shape and nothing else: a state, a render(), one listener.
  3. It's a classic script, not a module, so state and render() are reachable from the console. You'll need that at minute 15.
  4. Open your game's brief on a second laptop, for the people who aren't typing.

How you work

  • Design the state first (minutes 0–5), on paper: what the game has to remember, and what can be computed from that.
  • One person drives: types on the group's laptop.
  • Everyone else reviews and verifies: reads along, checks the brief, works through the edge cases.
  • Decide early who presents.

The six games (and two spares)

  • Game 1 · ★☆☆MemorySixteen cards face down. Find the eight pairs in as few moves as you can.
  • Game 2 · ★☆☆Tic-tac-toeTwo players, one screen, three in a row. The score carries over between rounds.
  • Game 3 · ★★☆WordleGuess the five-letter word in six tries. Green, yellow, grey.
  • Game 4 · ★★☆MinesweeperTen mines on a 9×9 board. Numbers, flags, and a first click that never loses.
  • Game 5 · ★★★2048Slide the tiles, merge equal ones, reach 2048.
  • Game 6 · ★★★SnakeIt moves on its own. You steer, it eats, it grows. Don't bite yourself.
  • Spare · ★☆☆Lights outA 5×5 grid of lights. Every click flips a plus shape. Turn them all off.
  • Spare · ★★☆SimonFour pads flash a sequence. Repeat it. Every round adds one more.

Everyone's stretch

Same for every game, once your must-haves work:

  • Undo: one step back, then another, as far as you like.
  • Save: close the tab mid-game, open it again, carry on.
  • Best score: kept across games and reloads.

The 45 minutes

0–5 · planWrite the state on paper. What is stored, what is computed?
5–15 · renderA hand-written state draws the board. No clicks yet. At 15: change state in the console, call render(), the board follows.
15–35 · rulesEvents change the state. Win and lose last.
35–42 · verifyEvery edge case in your brief. A new game mid-game.
42–45 · demoStop coding. Rehearse your 3 minutes once.

Presenting

Your 3 minutes
  1. Show the state first: type state in the console and walk through every key. Say what you chose not to store.
  2. Play it, and hit one edge case from your brief on purpose.
  3. One rule in code: the event, the state change, the render.
  4. One bug you hit, and how you found it.

The class asks one question per group. Good questions: "what happens if…?", "why is that in the state?", "what would you change with ten more minutes?"
