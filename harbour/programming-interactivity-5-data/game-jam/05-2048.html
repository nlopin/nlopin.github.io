Game jam · game 5

2048

A 4×4 board of numbered tiles. An arrow key slides every tile as far as it goes; two equal tiles that meet merge into their sum. After each move a new 2 (sometimes a 4) appears. Reach 2048. ★★★

“One more game. Just one.”

You'll demo: press ←: the tiles slide, equal ones merge, the score grows by the merged values, a new tile appears. Press a key that moves nothing: nothing happens, no new tile. A full board with no merge left: game over.

Must have (tick as you go)

What has to work, not how. The how is your call: you'll explain it on stage.

State

  • The sixteen cells as numbers.
  • The score.

Render

  • A 4×4 board drawn from state. Each value has its own colour.
  • The score, and a message for game over and for 2048.

Rules

  • All four arrow keys work.
  • A tile merges at most once per move.
  • A new tile appears only after a move that changed the board.

The one rule: the DOM is output. The numbers live in state, not in the tiles' text.

Edge cases (verify, minutes 35–42)

  • Every row in the table above, with ←. The same rows mirrored, with →.
  • The same as columns, with ↑ and ↓.
  • A full board that still has a merge is not game over.
  • The arrow keys don't scroll the page.

Stretch, if you're done early

  • Swipe on a phone (pointer events).
  • Keep playing after 2048.
  • Everyone's stretch: undo (the classic 2048 cheat), save, best score.

Stuck? One hint at a time

Try for five minutes first.

Hint 1

Can you get one row, one direction right, and test it in the console without any page at all?

Hint 2

Four directions. Do you need four different pieces of code?

Hint 3

"Did anything move?" How do you compare two boards in JavaScript?

Traps

Open when something behaves strangely
  • 2 2 2 2 becomes 8.
  • A new tile appears after every key press, even when nothing moved. Or never.
  • The page scrolls while you play.

Presenting

Your 3 minutes
  1. Show the state first: type state in the console and walk through every key. Say what you chose not to store.
  2. Play it, and hit one edge case from your brief on purpose.
  3. One rule in code: the event, the state change, the render.
  4. One bug you hit, and how you found it.
