Game jam · game 2

Tic-tac-toe

Two players, one screen. Three in a row wins, a full board with no line is a draw, and the score carries over from round to round. ★☆☆

“Best of five. Loser buys the coffee.”

You'll demo: X and O take turns. A win marks the three cells, stops the board and adds a point. A full board with no line says "Draw". Next round: an empty board, the same score.

Must have (tick as you go)

What has to work, not how. The how is your call: you'll explain it on stage.

State

  • The nine cells, and whose turn it is.
  • The score across rounds.

Render

  • A 3×3 board drawn from state.
  • A status line: whose turn, who won, or draw.
  • The score for X and O.

Rules

  • One listener for the board. Taken cells and a finished round ignore clicks.
  • Win and draw are worked out from the board.
  • Next round: empty board, score kept.

The one rule: the DOM is output. Nothing in your game logic reads the cells' text to find a winner.

Edge cases (verify, minutes 35–42)

  • Click a cell that's taken.
  • Click an empty cell after the round is won.
  • A line completed with the ninth move: is that a win or a draw?
  • Win five rounds in a row: the score says 5.
  • Play a whole round with Tab and Enter only.

Stretch, if you're done early

  • The player who didn't start this round starts the next one.
  • A computer player for O. First one that plays any free cell; then one that wins when it can and blocks when it must.
  • Everyone's stretch: undo, save, best score.

Stuck? One hint at a time

Try for five minutes first.

Hint 1

How many different lines win? Could they be data instead of a chain of ifs?

Hint 2

Is the winner something you have to store, or can you read it off the board at any moment?

Hint 3

The score changes once per round. Which of your functions runs exactly once per move, and which runs as often as it likes?

Traps

Open when something behaves strangely
  • The score goes up by more than one per win.
  • The ninth move completes a line, and the board says "Draw".
  • After a win, the losing player can still play on.

The 45 minutes

0–5 · planWrite the state on paper. What is stored, what is computed?
5–15 · renderA hand-written state draws the board. No clicks yet. At 15: change state in the console, call render(), the board follows.
15–35 · rulesEvents change the state. Win and lose last.
35–42 · verifyEvery edge case above. A new game mid-game.
42–45 · demoStop coding. Rehearse your 3 minutes once.

Presenting

Your 3 minutes
  1. Show the state first: type state in the console and walk through every key. Say what you chose not to store.
  2. Play it, and hit one edge case from your brief on purpose.
  3. One rule in code: the event, the state change, the render.
  4. One bug you hit, and how you found it.
