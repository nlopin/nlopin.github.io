Game jam · game 1

Memory

Sixteen cards face down, eight pairs. Turn two over: a pair stays up, a miss turns back. Find every pair in as few moves as you can. ★☆☆

“I know I saw the other octopus. Top left. Or was it…”

You'll demo: turn over a pair: it stays up. Turn over a miss: both cards turn back after a second, and a third click in that second does nothing. Finish the board: "Done in 14 moves". New game: a different layout.

Must have (tick as you go)

What has to work, not how. The how is your call: you'll explain it on stage.

State

  • The sixteen cards, and which of them are already matched.
  • Which cards are face up right now, and the number of moves.

Render

  • A 4×4 board drawn from state. Face down, face up and matched each look different.
  • The move count, and a win message once every pair is found.

Rules

  • One listener for all sixteen cards.
  • The second card decides: a pair stays up, a miss turns back after about a second.
  • New game: a fresh shuffle, moves back to zero.

The one rule: the DOM is output. Nothing in your game logic reads the page to find out which card is which.

New tool: Code that runs later: setTimeout. Not taught yet; MDN has what you need.

Edge cases (verify, minutes 35–42)

  • Click the same card twice.
  • Click a third card while two are waiting to turn back.
  • Click a card that's already matched.
  • Start a new game during the one-second wait.
  • Play a whole game with Tab and Enter only.

Stretch, if you're done early

  • A timer next to the moves. It starts on the first click, not on page load.
  • A 6×6 board, chosen before the game starts.
  • A flip animation. What happens to a CSS transition when render() replaces the element?
  • Everyone's stretch: undo, save, best score.

Stuck? One hint at a time

Try for five minutes first.

Hint 1

Is "the player has won" something you have to store?

Hint 2

Two octopus cards look the same. When you remember which cards are face up, how do you tell those two apart?

Hint 3

During the one-second wait, what should a click do? Where in the state can your listener find out that it's that second?

Traps

Open when something behaves strangely
  • A new game started during the wait: two cards of the new game turn over by themselves.
  • One card clicked twice counts as a pair.
  • The board works with the mouse but the keyboard can't reach the cards.

The 45 minutes

0–5 · planWrite the state on paper. What is stored, what is computed?
5–15 · renderA hand-written state draws the board. No clicks yet. At 15: change state in the console, call render(), the board follows.
15–35 · rulesEvents change the state. Win and lose last.
35–42 · verifyEvery edge case above. A new game mid-game.
42–45 · demoStop coding. Rehearse your 3 minutes once.

Presenting

Your 3 minutes
  1. Show the state first: type state in the console and walk through every key. Say what you chose not to store.
  2. Play it, and hit one edge case from your brief on purpose.
  3. One rule in code: the event, the state change, the render.
  4. One bug you hit, and how you found it.
