Game jam · spare

Lights out

A 5×5 grid of lights, some on. Clicking a light flips it and its four neighbours: up, down, left, right. Turn every light off. ★☆☆

“Every time I fix one corner, the other one lights up.”

You'll demo: click a light in the middle: a plus shape of five flips. Click a corner: only three flip. Turn them all off: "Solved in 9 moves". New puzzle: a different one, and it can be solved.

Must have (tick as you go)

What has to work, not how. The how is your call: you'll explain it on stage.

State

  • The 25 lights, on or off.
  • The number of moves.

Render

  • A 5×5 board drawn from state. On and off are obvious at a glance.
  • The move count, and a message when every light is off.

Rules

  • One listener for the board. A click flips the light and its existing neighbours.
  • New puzzle: random, and always solvable.
  • Once solved, clicks do nothing until the next puzzle.

The one rule: the DOM is output. Nothing in your game logic reads a cell's class to know whether it's lit.

Edge cases (verify, minutes 35–42)

  • Click each corner: three lights flip. Each edge: four.
  • Click the same light twice: the board is back where it was.
  • Ten new puzzles: none of them starts already solved.
  • Play a whole puzzle with Tab and Enter only.

Stretch, if you're done early

  • Levels: the first puzzles are easy, later ones harder. What makes a puzzle hard?
  • A puzzle in the URL: send a link, your friend gets the same board.
  • Everyone's stretch: undo, save, best score (fewest moves per puzzle).

Stuck? One hint at a time

Try for five minutes first.

Hint 1

Is "solved" something you have to store?

Hint 2

Not every random pattern of lights can be turned off. Is there a way to make a puzzle you already know is solvable?

Hint 3

Which neighbours does a cell on the right edge have? How does your code know it's on the edge?

Traps

Open when something behaves strangely
  • Clicking on the right edge flips a light on the left edge, one row down.
  • A puzzle nobody in the group can solve.

The 45 minutes

0–5 · planWrite the state on paper. What is stored, what is computed?
5–15 · renderA hand-written state draws the board. No clicks yet. At 15: change state in the console, call render(), the board follows.
15–35 · rulesEvents change the state. Win and lose last.
35–42 · verifyEvery edge case above. A new game mid-game.
42–45 · demoStop coding. Rehearse your 3 minutes once.

Presenting

Your 3 minutes
  1. Show the state first: type state in the console and walk through every key. Say what you chose not to store.
  2. Play it, and hit one edge case from your brief on purpose.
  3. One rule in code: the event, the state change, the render.
  4. One bug you hit, and how you found it.
