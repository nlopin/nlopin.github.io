Exercise 2 · Six frames

Storyboard an interactive explanation of one idea, on paper, before any code. Six frames, a sketch and a sentence each. You'll build the middle three after the break.

Pick an idea (1 min)

Your own, or one of these. Each is a claim that surprises someone who hasn't seen it:

  • A hash table finds a key without looking at the other keys.
  • Bubble sort is fine for 10 items and useless for 100 000.
  • Binary search finds a word among a million in 20 steps.
  • Array(3).fill([]) holds the same inner array three times.
  • [10, 9, 1].sort() returns [1, 10, 9].
  • Clients that retry at fixed intervals after an outage all hit the server at the same moment; random jitter spreads them out.
  • 0.1 + 0.2 !== 0.3, and a shop's totals drift by a cent.
  • Raising a cache hit rate from 90% to 99% cuts the slow requests tenfold, not by 9%.

Rule: write the idea as one sentence with a verb. "Sorting" is a topic. "Comparing strings puts 10 before 9" is an idea.

Draw six frames (7 min)

On the printable sheet or six boxes on any paper. A filled-in example: the token bucket, as it was storyboarded before it was built.

  1. The question. What the reader sees first, and why they should care.
  2. The first touch. The one thing they can change. What happens when they do?
  3. The second control. What it is, and the "but" or "therefore" that brings it in now and not earlier.
  4. The surprise. What the reader sees that they didn't expect. Which default makes it happen?
  5. The rule. The general statement, in one sentence of text.
  6. The ending. The question the reader leaves with, or the sandbox they get.

Swap (6 min, 3 each way)

Give your sheet to your neighbour. They answer these about your storyboard, in writing, on the sheet:

  • What question does it open with? Would I care?
  • How many seconds until I can touch something?
  • Where is the "but" or "therefore" between frames 2 and 3?
  • How does it end: a sandbox, a summary, a question?

Traps

  • Frame 1 is a definition. A definition answers a question nobody has asked yet.
  • Frame 2 offers every control at once: a dashboard, not a first touch.
  • The surprise depends on luck (random data). Write down the input that makes it happen every time.
  • No frame states the rule in words. Someone who never touches anything should still learn it.
