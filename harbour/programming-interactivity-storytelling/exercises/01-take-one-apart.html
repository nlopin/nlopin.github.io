Act 1 · the shape of an explanation

Exercise 1 · Take one apart

Each group gets one interactive explanation. Use it as a reader for 12 minutes, then spend 8 minutes taking it apart with the card below. The goal is the author's decisions, not the topic: what you were shown first, what you were allowed to touch, and in what order.

How

  • 0–12 min: read and play, each on your own laptop. Don't discuss yet. Note the moment you first understood something, and the moment you got lost.
  • 12–20 min: fill in one card per group: on paper (printable card) or in a shared doc.
  • Report back: one sentence per group. What surprised you, and what made the surprise possible?

The card

  1. The idea in one sentence. (A sentence, not a topic: "a line moves toward the points it gets wrong", not "machine learning".)
  2. What question does it open with? Did you care about it by the end of the first screen?
  3. How many seconds until the first thing you can touch? What is it?
  4. Which controls exist at the start, and which appear later?
  5. Find one "but" and one "therefore" in the sequence of sections.
  6. How many views of the same state are on screen at once? Does changing one change the others?
  7. Where were you surprised? What made the surprise possible?
  8. Scroll through it again without touching anything. What's lost?
  9. How does it end: a sandbox, a summary, a question?

Traps

  • Retelling the topic ("it's about how GPS works") instead of the author's decisions. The card asks about the page, not the subject.
  • Card question 3 answered with a guess. Reload and count.
  • A page that doesn't load or stutters: take a spare, and note it. A heavy page is a decision too.
